Monte Carlo — 2,000 bootstrapped trade orderings 5th–95th & 25th–75th percentile equity cones + median
What this is: we take the strategy's actual realised trade returns (as a share of the portfolio) and resample them 2,000 times with replacement. Each resample is one plausible "alternate history" — the same kinds of trades in a different mix and order. The shaded cones show where the middle 50% (25th–75th) and 90% (5th–95th) of those paths end up. It answers "how much does the headline result depend on the particular mix and sequence of trades?" — it is not a forecast of future prices.

About this dashboard

What this is

A dashboard depicting a leveraged Nasdaq-100 trend-following swing strategy, backtested and presented with a live signal monitor. It was built primarily with Claude Code, on top of the author’s own TradingView Pine research into TQQQ swing systems developed over several years. The engine is Python; the dashboard is a single self-contained page, and the whole pipeline updates itself in the cloud.

Why it was built

This is a personal quantitative research project, intended for demonstration and educational purposes only: to show the strategy’s logic, its backtest, and how its signals are read day to day. It also exists to explore what modern AI coding tools — in particular Claude Code — can do when pointed at a real quantitative-finance problem end to end: the data pipeline, the signal engine, the backtest and this live front-end were all built collaboratively with AI. It is a personal project, not a product or a service. More of the author’s work can be found on his portfolio webpage.

How to read it

Live Chart & Signals — TQQQ price with entry/exit markers plus the live signal monitor (hover or click any bar to see exactly what the strategy saw that day). P&L Summary — equity curve, performance stats and the drawdown-recovery profile. Trades Detail — every closed trade plus the current open position, with the rule that closed it and where the reading sat versus its threshold. Risk Analysis — Monte Carlo, rolling returns, MAE/MFE. Markets & Regime — risk/return versus buy-and-hold and performance by market regime. Forward-Looking Analysis — finds the past days whose signal readings most resembled today's and shows what followed: a fan of the next ten sessions across those analogs, day-by-day odds, the outcome distribution at a chosen horizon, and the closest individual matches. These are historical frequencies among similar days, not forecasts. About — this page: how the strategy works and how to read the dashboard.

Data & updates

Quotes and the volatility readings come from public sources (Yahoo intraday, CBOE end-of-day files, FRED) and are delayed (~5–15 min). Everything refreshes automatically in the cloud — live quotes and candles roughly every five minutes through the session, and the historical signals after each close — so the page stays current without anything running locally.

Limitations

Data is provided as-is with no guarantee of accuracy, completeness or availability — feeds can lag or fail, and a failed refresh leaves the last good values on screen. The backtest trades the real TQQQ and SQQQ price histories, so the funds’ fees and daily-reset decay are already reflected; it fills at the next market open with 5 bps of slippage per side, but excludes commissions and taxes, and assumes the extra capital for the leverage boost is available at no financing cost. The rules and thresholds were refined on this same 2011–2026 history, so the results are in-sample and are best read as an optimistic upper bound rather than an expectation; a 15-year backtest of a 3× instrument also leans heavily on one long bull-market era. The strategy has not been traded live at scale.

About this strategy

The idea

This is a trend-following swing strategy on the Nasdaq-100, traded through two 3× leveraged ETFs: TQQQ (3× long) in uptrends, SQQQ (3× inverse) in confirmed downtrends, and cash only in the brief transitions between them (~8% of days). It began life as a TradingView Pine Script strategy developed by the author of this dashboard. The strategy was built on an adaptive Ichimoku core plus a broad set of supporting indicators, then ported to Python on daily bars and tuned into the configuration shown here.

When it buys TQQQ

When TQQQ closes back above its 20-day EMA and the VIX curve is not in deep backwardation. That is the whole entry. Testing showed that adding a battery of additional oscillator filters to the long side systematically kept the strategy in cash during rebounds — the market’s best days — so the entry is kept deliberately simple. Easy entry, regime-gated grip.

When it buys SQQQ

Only in a genuine bear scenario: the close below its 20-day EMA, the three oversold blockers clear, plus two filters added in the Python rework — QQQ below its 200-day SMA (the regime gate) and the VIX curve not in steep contango. Bear entries are deliberately rare (15 of 47 backtest trades), sized at reduced size, and carry both a trailing stop and a hard stop, because rallies inside downtrends are violent.

How it exits

Structurally and regime-gated — never on strength. While QQQ holds above its 200-day SMA, TQQQ rides through dips, oscillator extremes and even bearish Ichimoku bars; only a VXN crash-spike (an extreme percentile reading) or a deep catastrophe trail can force it out. Once QQQ loses the 200-day, the Ichimoku structure takes over: a bearish price bar or bearish Chikou closes the position at the next open. Exiting a strong up-trend on ordinary “overbought” oscillator readings was found to surrender most of the compounding, so the long side is left to run and only a genuine trend break closes it. SQQQ exits on exhaustion — a stochastic-oversold or ATR-collapse reading — or on its trailing stop or hard stop.

Timing & execution

Signals are evaluated on the daily close (confirmed once CBOE posts the day’s end-of-day prints, ~5:30 PM ET) and the resulting order executes at the next market open. No intraday “repaint” — a TradingView term for an indicator that quietly rewrites its own past values as new data arrives, flattering a backtest with hindsight it never had. Here every decision uses only the confirmed daily close and fills at that same next open with slippage modelled, so what the backtest shows is what could actually have been traded.

Position sizing

TQQQ trades at full size, and levers up once a position has given back a large share of its gains from its highest close without an exit firing, holding the boost until a new peak close. The deep trigger is deliberate: shallower pullbacks often keep falling, while give-backs this deep have historically been followed by the sharpest rebounds — and the trigger never deepened the strategy’s worst drawdown. SQQQ trades at reduced size. Cash is not a position the strategy seeks — it appears only when neither side qualifies.
